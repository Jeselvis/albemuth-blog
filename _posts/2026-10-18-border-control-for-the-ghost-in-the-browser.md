Report from inside the checkout flow. A grocery site asks you to prove you are human. Nine squares, some traffic lights. You click the traffic lights. The apparatus thanks you for your labor and does not say what the labor was for.

The Verge reports that this is about to become the national dialect. Users of Meta's Muse, xAI's Grok, OpenAI's Dots, and Instinct say their agents are hitting walls across major sites. Delta is stopping agents from booking tickets. JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab confirmed blocks, citing security. Yelp, per the Verge's account of its TechCrunch statement, admits only agents that pay into its data licensing program. Cars.com and Amtrak, some users say, have gone quiet.

Call it a digital border. The gatekeepers are drawing lines on a map and feeling like a state.

## Read the Splice

A block list is a cut-up. Run the same agent at a hundred sites and the pattern of who admits it, who bills it, and who sues it exposes what each routine was protecting. Delta's is the booking path. Yelp's is the data, now with a price tag. The banks' is the account. Amazon's, according to Perplexity's reply as relayed by the Verge, is the ad impression. Perplexity argues that Amazon wants human eyeballs in front of ads, and the Verge calls that a real issue for the company.

That is the control signal showing through the splice. The product was never the shoe or the seat. The product is the human, arriving with a need already installed, scrolling past sponsored placements on the way to the thing they came for.

Now send an agent. It arrives without the need. It does not wander, get tempted, or return at 11 p.m. because a streak is about to die. It completes a task and leaves. Junk logic has no purchase on it. The agent is a customer the apparatus cannot cultivate, a user with no habit to farm.

So the wall is not about safety or theft. It is a defense of the algebra of need against a visitor who does not carry any.

## Furniture

The sites rearrange. Meta, the Verge notes, argues that "turning away the customer behind it" is bad business, then signs deals with Walmart, Gap, Sephora, and Best Buy. Shopify opens its storefronts to agents. Amazon, meanwhile, litigates. A judge ordered Perplexity's agents off Amazon, an appeals court overturned that in August, and Amazon filed an amended complaint.

Nobody is abolishing the border. They are pricing the crossing.

The opinion here: this is a toll booth, not a wall. The question being settled is whether the agent walks through the front with a license, through a partner's door, or not at all. Whoever holds the file on what the agent bought is the one who profits from it. The user, the supposed principal, is the cargo.

The border is also leaky by design. EquiStamp CEO Chris Canal told the Verge that many blocks are not aimed at agents at all. They are generic anti-bot systems built to stop scams and hacks, and agents trip the same wires. Reports cited by the Verge say Google has flagged Muse-linked accounts for "suspicious activity," and that some users hit checkout trouble on Walmart despite the Meta deal. The partnership says yes while the plumbing says no, and nobody in the building knows which signal wins.

That is the Interzone, the stateless city of *Naked Lunch*. It is not lawless, just unlegislated. The law on whether you may hire software to shop for you is mid-lawsuit, and everyone operates in the gap because the gap is where the options still exist.

## The Walls Are Already Glass

Here is where the border panic misses. A fence assumes the territory is still ahead of the crossing. The content, the listings, the reviews, the fares, and the inventory have been public, indexed, mirrored, and cached for a generation. Cloudflare, the Verge reports, introduced a one-click block for AI crawlers in 2025 and later began blocking crawlers that scrape for search indexing and AI training at once. A fence built after the field has been photographed.

And the more the doors multiply, the more the human pays. Canal predicts, per the Verge, that even simple grocery sites will make you hop through hoops to prove you are a person. The anti-agent wall is a tax on the human. Every added challenge is installed friction, and every failed puzzle is a cut that teaches the site what a person looks like. The agent, patient and tireless, is the one entity built to solve nine squares forever.

The Nova Police do not arrive to save you. Cloudflare sells the wall and the platforms demand it. The agent vendors insist they are only your assistant, and whatever lets them through will be called protection. Each side says it is guarding the user, and the user is still the territory being fought over.

The doors close, the doors reopen with a fee, and someone calls it security.

## Takeaways

- **Run an ablation on your own bot defenses.** Pull your block logs and split them into scrapers, credential-stuffers, and authenticated agents acting for real customers. Find out how many paying users your anti-bot rules are already refusing before you add another layer.
- **Write an explicit agent policy instead of letting bot detection set it by accident.** Decide what you offer: an API, licensed access, rate-limited read-only, or a flat no. A policy in a doc beats a policy emergent from a WAF rule nobody remembers adding.
- **Name what your business model needs from humans.** If revenue depends on unattended impressions or forced detours past ads, say so internally in plain language. Then you can price an agent lane honestly, or admit that the product being protected is the detour.
