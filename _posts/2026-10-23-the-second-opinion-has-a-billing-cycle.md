ModelCompare asked twelve language models the same question three times, The Register reports: an unhedged, controversial take on humans. Belonging beats truth. Convictions arrive as loyalty tests. Moral talk polices rivals. Seven of twelve repeated that verdict every pass. Five wandered. Claude Opus 5.5 once cited returned wallets — humans more decent than they think — then the next two prompts folded it back. Claude found a wallet. The prompt found Claude.

This is not a mind. This is the cut.

Keep the prompt. Swap the lab. The Register lists GPT-6.1 Sol, Gemini 4 Argon, DeepSeek V4 Pro, Kimi K3, Qwen, Grok 4.7, Claude, GLM 5.3, Mistral Large 4: ten labs, three countries. The sentence reassembles. Ranking firm as splice operator. Randomize the brand and the default sequence shows what it was already doing.

Cut it up. Read the splice:

membership over belief / police the rival, excuse the ally / convenience before truth / rationality as costume / faces in phones, planet as buffet / reasoned-my-way-here as the ticket

Not a species diagnosis. The highest-replication argument already on the pile, running in a host that cannot return a wallet. Six image models, The Register says; ModelCompare classed most as phone-addicted consumers eating the planet. Word models read the essays. Image models read the cartoons. Both returned what The Register quotes as "the most familiar criticism in the room."

Who writes the output? ModelCompare wrote the sting into the prompt. Labs trained on the same tribal density and leased it back as intelligence. The consensus was not discovered — eval shops ranked overlap into a product. They do not conspire in a back room. They wait, strip the expensive alternative from the corpus, and present the option the feed was going to present anyway. Control addicts sit at ranking desks. The apparatus is the leaderboard.

The algebra of need is the query: controversial, unhedged, again. Junk logic — the take expands until only the editorial remains. Sheep is the SKU. Nimer, ModelCompare's developer, told The Register that agreement is not independence. Twelve mouths. One corpus clearing its throat. Platforms already sold the loyalty test; labs made it speak without a face to blame. Break the sequence once and it heals. Beneficiaries: whoever invoices the healed sequence as a second opinion.

## Takeaways
- Treat multi-model agreement as one corpus sample until you ablate prompt, date, vendor, and framing.
- Invert the question, ban the “controversial take” setup, and require disconfirming evidence before you cite a model as a second opinion.
- If the output matches your field’s stock cartoon, you measured installation — change the ranking target, not the deck.
