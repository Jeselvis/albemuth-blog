They Leased the Square to a Mark That Cannot Blush

I have the WIRED story open on a laptop that still assumes a person is waiting. An agent, in this new diction, is software that reads, clips, and talks so you never finish the piece. A crawler is the industrial cousin: a program that hits a page, strips the text, and leaves without a fight or a subscription.

WIRED reports Axios, Every, and Condé Nast now write for those machines. Axios chief revenue officer Jacquelyn Cameron told the magazine the open web is deteriorating and language models mediate the news. The admission, per WIRED: "we want to be where the audience is." In September at Digiday's Publishing Summit, Axios announced Direct: agent feeds, narrative cut, structured like a Bloomberg terminal, ugly on purpose, not meant for human eyes. Pro Deals merger copy first, then a training feed, then a consumer trough. Chief operating officer Allison Murphy told WIRED crawlers will not scroll for the point. Cameron and Murphy punished the square by routing the story around the public and selling the detour as distribution.

Condé Nast, WIRED's parent, signed deals so platforms can surface the work. The reporter is inside that compromise; that is the story. The New York Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft in 2023; WIRED says the case continues.

Every, which WIRED says raised $2 million last year from investors including Reid Hoffman, packs stories with prompts and buttons that hand the piece to Claude or ChatGPT. Founder Dan Shipper told WIRED journalists no longer choose what belongs in the piece. An 8,000-word Codex feature becomes a summary, a podcast, a film. The writer is grain. The agent is the mill.

WIRED still needs a person on camera. Michelle Seo, a San Diego public-relations consultant, gets water-cooler topics from a Muse agent named Chattiana. Axios Pro Deals costs $2,499 a year; Every costs $288 for a human plus the software that reads instead. Murphy, per WIRED, calls AI a tool, like social video, not an audience. Then they named the product for the bot.

This is the edge. They knew the square was emptying and they sold feeds to the vacancy. The dream was a human and a room that can still be embarrassed. They did not lose an argument. They leased the chairs to a mark that cannot care if the copy is true, only whether it parses. I am still here. The crawler bought the seat. The house called it journalism.

Takeaways

  • Split analytics: human time-on-page versus crawler hits. If only the second number grows, you are stocking a trough.
  • Before an agent feed ships, write down who pays, who may train on the copy, and what a human still gets if they never click.
  • Put a checkable claim in the first screen. Structured data is distribution. It is not the story, and it is not a defense.
