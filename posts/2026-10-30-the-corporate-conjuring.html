The Corporate Conjuring

They are trying to embalm the ghost. The great enterprise push for a “context layer” is not operational hygiene; it is necromantic accounting. Its goal is to bottle the animating spirit of the organization—the whispers in the hallways, the unspoken tribal knowledge, the heuristics born from a thousand failures—and distill it into governed, machine-usable packets. They call this “making AI useful.” It is a death rite. You are not integrating AI. You are performing the Babalon Working for the C-suite: invoking a new archetype—the Perfect, Legible Employee—into the corporate sphere, and waiting to see what shows up.

According to the MIT Technology Review article cited, fewer than one in five organizations extract transformative value from AI. Most are just piloting or assisting. This is not a failure of technology, but of invocation. They have summoned immense capability—Prukalpa Sankar, co-founder of Atlan, notes in the article that for practical purposes, “artificial general intelligence is already here”—but without the correct, consecrated context, it is a blindfolded genius intern, as Sankar says. The apparatus is live, but the sigil is wrong. The working is underway, but the will is confused. Is the goal to serve the business, or to *become* a frozen, queryable snapshot of the business? The gap between model capability and organizational utility, noted in the article, is The Abyss where all unexamined hype narratives go to die.

They seek to codify intuition into weights because a process nobody can explain is theater, and theater is a liability. Anecdotal grievance is a vulnerability. Institutional memory that walks out the door at 5 PM is a risk. The context layer is the ultimate banishing ritual for the human mess. But a ghost stuffed into a digital box is still a ghost. And nobody has written the postmortem for when it decides to haunt the ledger.

Takeaways

* Treat your "context layer" as a live summoning, not a data dump. Map not just what knowledge *is*, but what it *does* and what its removal would break. * The "smartest intern, blindfolded" analogy from the cited article is a safety warning. Your pre-flight checklist for any AI deployment must include: "Have we defined the Will of this working beyond 'be useful'?" * If your context-capture process feels like taxidermy, it is. Prioritize encoding decision *logs* and *rationales* over static facts, so the model can learn the ritual, not just the relic.
